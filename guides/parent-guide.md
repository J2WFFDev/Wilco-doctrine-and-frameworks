# Parent Guide

---

## Welcome

Your athlete is part of a program that is about far more than shooting. We are building discipline, focus, resilience, and character. You are a critical partner in that work.

This guide explains your role, our expectations, and how to support your athlete most effectively.

---

## Your Role

Parents at Wilco are supporters — not coaches, not competition managers, not scorekeepers.

**You are responsible for:**
- Getting your athlete to practice and matches on time and prepared
- Ensuring equipment is properly maintained and stored safely at home
- Reinforcing the values of effort, honesty, and composure outside the program
- Modeling the sportsmanship we expect from athletes
- Communicating concerns directly to the coach

**Please leave to the coach:**
- Technical instruction at the range
- Feedback during and after strings
- Goal-setting and performance review

Coaching from the sideline — even well-intentioned — creates confusion and undermines the athlete's ability to internalize their own process.

---

## Match Day

On match day, your job is simple: **be a calm, supportive presence.**

- Let your athlete manage their own preparation
- Do not comment on stage results in real time
- Celebrate effort and composure, not just scores
- After the match, let the coach debrief the athlete first
- Your conversation at home can reinforce what the coach addressed — it should not contradict or override it

---

## Communication

- Bring concerns to the coach privately — not at the range during practice or competition.
- Questions about your athlete's development are always welcome. Schedule a conversation.
- If you observe a safety concern, address it immediately with the coach or range officer — this is the one exception where immediate communication is always appropriate.

---

## Safety

You share responsibility for safe equipment handling and storage at home.

- Firearms must be stored securely and inaccessible to unauthorized users at all times.
- Ammunition must be stored separately from firearms.
- Know where your athlete's equipment is and that it is properly secured.

---

## Program Culture

We ask parents to support the culture we are building:

- Affirm your athlete's effort and process, not just their results
- Talk positively about competitors and officials
- Avoid comparing your athlete to other athletes publicly
- Trust the coach's process, even when progress feels slow
