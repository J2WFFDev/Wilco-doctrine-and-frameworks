# Coach Guide

> **Canonical terminology reference:** [doctrine/terminology.md](../doctrine/terminology.md)

---

## Role of the Coach

The coach is the architect of the athlete's development environment — not a result-manager or a cheerleader. The coach:

- Designs practice with purpose
- Gives feedback that is specific, observable, and actionable
- Teaches the athlete to self-assess
- Holds the standard with consistency and patience
- Represents the program in every interaction

---

## Session Planning

Every training session should have:

1. **Defined objective** — What specific performance component is being developed?
2. **Drill selection** — Which drills from the [drill catalog](../drills/steel-drill-catalog.md) target that component?
3. **Measurement method** — How will progress be observed and recorded?
4. **Review plan** — How will the session be closed and what will athletes log?

Avoid "just shooting." Unstructured repetition builds inconsistency.

---

## Giving Feedback

Effective feedback is:

- **Specific** — "Your trigger finger was moving before the sight was on target" is useful. "Be more careful" is not.
- **Observable** — Based on what you saw, not what you assumed.
- **Actionable** — The athlete must be able to do something different with the feedback.
- **Timely** — Given immediately after the observable event when possible.

**Feedback cadence:**
- Give feedback on one thing at a time. More than one change creates cognitive overload.
- Confirm the athlete understood by asking them to restate it.
- Follow up in the next string — did the behavior change?

---

## Using the PARR Cycle

Structure every drill iteration around [PARR](../doctrine/terminology.md#parr):

| Phase | Coach Action |
|-------|-------------|
| **Prepare** | Confirm the athlete has a plan before approaching the line |
| **Act** | Observe, do not interrupt |
| **Review** | Ask the athlete to self-assess first, then add your observation |
| **Repeat** | Confirm the intent for the next string |

The goal is to make the athlete do the PARR cycle independently. Eventually they should not need you to prompt it.

---

## Performance Review

After every match, conduct a structured review:

1. Have the athlete classify each run (clean, sloppy, recovery, miss)
2. Ask: "What was the cause of the result on that string?"
3. Identify the single highest-leverage variable to adjust
4. Set the intent for the next training session

Avoid post-match conversations that are only emotional or only outcome-focused. Bring both in balance.

---

## Parent Communication

- Communicate proactively. If an athlete is struggling, parents should hear it from you before they hear it from the athlete.
- Keep parent feedback conversations separate from athlete training time.
- Be consistent in messaging between what you tell the athlete and what you tell the parent.
- If a parent is interfering with athlete development (sideline coaching, outcome pressure), address it directly and early. See [guides/parent-guide.md](parent-guide.md).

---

## Document and Record Keeping

- Log each athlete's session performance notes
- Track OKR progress across the season
- Note equipment changes and their effect on performance
- Maintain safety incident records per [operations/safety-manual.md](../operations/safety-manual.md)
