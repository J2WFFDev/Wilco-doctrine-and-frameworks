## Coaching Role Types at Wilco

Wilco Shooting Sports uses distinct coaching roles depending on context. Understanding what role you are filling — and what that role does and does not include — prevents overlap and confusion.

| Role | Context | Primary Focus |
|------|---------|--------------|
| **Head Coach** | Program-wide | Coach development, program culture, athlete development strategy |
| **Practice Coach** | Training sessions | Skill development, drill execution, feedback, PARR facilitation |
| **Match Coach** | Competition day | Athlete support, stage strategy, emotional management |

A single person may fill multiple roles, but the responsibilities are distinct and should not be mixed during the relevant context.

---

## Role of the Match Coach

The Match Coach is present to support the athlete on competition day — not to fix technique, not to evaluate overall development, and not to manage range operations.

The Match Coach's job is to:

1. Help athletes arrive at the line prepared and composed
2. Support stage planning and walk-through
3. Provide between-stage emotional support and recalibration
4. Recognize when an athlete is struggling emotionally and respond appropriately
5. Conduct a brief post-match review focused on process, not outcome

## What the Match Coach Does NOT Do

- Does not attempt major technique corrections during competition
- Does not override the athlete's stage plan after execution has begun
- Does not communicate with RSOs or Match Director on competitive rulings
- Does not interact with scoring — that is the Scorekeeper's role
- Does not second-guess or contradict the Head Coach's development strategy on match day

> *Rule: Match day is not a teaching day. It is an execution day. Coaching on match day should support execution, not generate new information that competes with what the athlete already knows.*
