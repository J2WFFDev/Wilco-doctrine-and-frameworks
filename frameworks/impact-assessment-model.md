# Impact Assessment Model

## Purpose

Impact assessment is the real-time determination of whether a shot hit its intended target. The method used to make that determination — and when it is made — has a direct effect on both accuracy and speed.

This model defines the five primary impact assessment modes and the coaching framework for developing and deploying each one appropriately.

> **See also:** [Impact Assessment definition in terminology.md](../doctrine/terminology.md#impact-assessment)

---

## Why This Matters

Most coaching programs treat impact assessment as a single, binary skill: "did you see the hit?" The reality is more nuanced.

At higher performance levels, the specific *type* of assessment being used determines:
- When the decision to transition occurs
- How much time is consumed in the engagement zone
- What happens when the expected signal is missing or ambiguous

Understanding and developing each assessment mode intentionally is what separates systematic skill development from guesswork.

## The Five Assessment Modes

### 1. Visual Assessment

**Definition:** The shooter confirms a hit by directly observing the target reaction — typically the steel falling, rocking, or showing a visible response to impact.

**How it works:**
- Eyes track the target after the shot break
- Confirmation comes from visible target movement
- Transition decision is made on visual confirmation

**When it is appropriate:**
- Foundation-level athletes learning the engagement process
- Stages with high stop-plate risk where visual confirmation is worth extra time
- Situations where audible feedback is masked or unreliable (outdoor ranges, wind, hearing protection variation)

**Coaching notes:**
- The most intuitive and universally applicable mode
- Slowest when used in isolation at advanced levels
- Should be developed first before introducing other modes

**Common errors:**
- Staying in visual assessment too long at advanced levels (reduces speed unnecessarily)
- Visual confirmation delay — pausing to watch the fall before transitioning

---

### 2. Audible Assessment

**Definition:** The shooter confirms a hit by hearing the impact sound — the distinctive ring, ping, or tone of a successful steel hit.

**How it works:**
- The shooter recognizes the acoustic signature of a solid hit
- Transition decision is made on the audible cue, without waiting for visual confirmation
- Eyes may already be moving to the next target when the sound arrives

**When it is appropriate:**
- Intermediate and Senior athletes with established mechanical fundamentals
- Ranges and conditions where steel acoustics are clear and consistent
- When combined with partial visual confirmation as a hybrid mode

**Coaching notes:**
- Audible assessment enables faster transitions because sound arrives faster than visual processing of target movement
- Requires familiarity with the acoustic signature of hits vs. misses on the specific equipment in use
- Can fail in loud environments, with suppressed firearms, or with non-standard target materials

**Common errors:**
- Confusing a near-miss or glancing hit sound for a solid hit
- Transitioning on the sound of the shot rather than the sound of the impact
- Environmental dependency — only works on familiar ranges

---

### 3. Predictive Assessment

**Definition:** The shooter fires based on confidence that the sight picture and execution are sufficient to produce a hit — without waiting for visual or audible confirmation.

**How it works:**
- Shot is fired as the muzzle arrives on target, based on internalized target geometry
- Assessment is pre-shot: "I know where the target is, I know my presentation is correct, I will fire now"
- Confirmation is not required before transitioning

**When it is appropriate:**
- Senior athletes with excellent presentation consistency
- High-value time situations — typically the open field or larger targets
- Stages where the athlete's personal accuracy data supports predictive engagement

**Coaching notes:**
- This is the fastest mode — it eliminates confirmation time entirely
- It is also the highest-risk mode — misses are not caught until after transition
- Should be earned through data: an athlete who predictively executes should have the hit rate to justify it
- **Not appropriate for stop plates** — a predictive miss on the stop plate typically costs more time than the confirmation saves

**Common errors:**
- Using predictive execution before consistent presentation mechanics are established
- Treating predictive execution as a shortcut rather than an earned skill
- Applying predictive mode to stop plates (high-risk, rarely worth it)

---

### 4. Index / Feel / Force-of-Will Assessment

**Definition:** A proprioceptive or kinesthetic confirmation mode in which the shooter has internalized the physical sensation of a correct shot well enough to assess impact through feel rather than external sensory data.

**How it works:**
- The shooter registers whether the shot felt "right" — grip, stance, sight picture, trigger press, and recoil all aligned with the internalized model of a good shot
- Assessment comes from body sensation and shot execution quality, not from observing the result

**When it is appropriate:**
- Highly experienced athletes
- Situations where external feedback is absent or delayed
- As a secondary confirmation channel in hybrid assessment

**Coaching notes:**
- This is an advanced and fragile mode — it depends on a well-calibrated internal reference model
- Athletes who have not logged sufficient clean repetitions may have an incorrect internal reference
- Index/feel assessment can produce high confidence in a fundamentally broken shot if the athlete has learned the wrong pattern
- Periodic calibration against visual/audible feedback is essential to keep the internal model accurate

**Common errors:**
- Developing feel-based confidence before the underlying mechanics are clean (confident errors)
- Using feel as the primary mode without periodic external calibration
- Confusing "comfortable" with "correct"

---

### 5. Hybrid Assessment

**Definition:** The simultaneous or sequential use of two or more assessment modes within a single engagement, or across different engagements within a stage.

**How it works:**
- The athlete selects or automatically deploys the appropriate assessment mode for each target
- A common pattern: visual on the stop plate, audible/predictive on open field targets
- Modes can be mixed within a string as the situation demands

**When it is appropriate:**
- Intermediate and Senior athletes who have developed multiple modes
- Any stage where different targets carry different risk levels or time values
- Athletes performing at competitive level where assessment mode selection is tactical

**Coaching notes:**
- Hybrid is not a separate skill — it is the flexible application of developed modes
- The most effective competitors are not locked into one mode; they deploy the right mode for the right situation
- Teaching hybrid assessment begins by making the distinction between modes explicit, then allowing athletes to select deliberately

**Common errors:**
- Mode confusion — accidentally applying the wrong assessment mode to a target
- Inconsistent mode application across strings (different assessment strategy on identical targets)
- Over-engineering mode selection in competition instead of letting trained instincts operate

## Assessment Mode by Development Level

| Mode | Foundation | Intermediate | Senior |
|------|-----------|-------------|--------|
| Visual | Primary | Available | Available |
| Audible | Introduced | Developing | Primary (open field) |
| Predictive | Not recommended | Introduced cautiously | Available (earned) |
| Index/Feel | Not applicable | Early awareness | Available |
| Hybrid | Not applicable | Emerging | Primary approach |

---

## Coaching Decision Framework

When an athlete misses a target, understanding which assessment mode was in use helps diagnose the cause:

| Assessment Mode in Use | Likely Error Source |
|------------------------|-------------------|
| Visual | Trigger jerk, anticipation, rushed visual confirmation |
| Audible | Environmental masking, acoustic confusion, miss-sound misidentified |
| Predictive | Presentation breakdown, poor target geometry, premature deployment of this mode |
| Index/Feel | Miscalibrated internal reference, incorrect fundamental learned as "correct" |
| Hybrid | Mode selection error, inappropriate mode for that target type |
