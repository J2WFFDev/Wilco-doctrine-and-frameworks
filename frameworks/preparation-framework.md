# Preparation Framework

## Purpose

Preparation is not pre-performance anxiety management. It is the structured process of building the conditions under which execution can succeed.

Most athlete development programs focus almost entirely on execution. The Wilco model treats preparation as an equally trainable skill — one that is developmentally layered, tool-supported, and coached deliberately.

> **See also:** Preparation terms in [doctrine/terminology.md](../doctrine/terminology.md): Stage Preparation, String Preparation, PARR

---

## The Three Preparation Layers

Preparation at Wilco is organized into three nested layers, each with a different time horizon and purpose.

```
MATCH PREPARATION      ← Days to hours before
  └── STAGE PREPARATION    ← Minutes before
        └── STRING PREPARATION  ← Seconds before
```

These layers are sequential and cumulative. Quality at the outer layer (Match Prep) creates the foundation for quality at the inner layers. An athlete who arrives at a match unprepared cannot compensate for that gap with superior string prep.

---

## Layer 1 — Match Preparation

**Time horizon:** Hours to days before the match.

**Purpose:** Arrive at the venue physically ready, mentally settled, and logistically organized.

Match preparation addresses everything that happens before the athlete steps onto the range.

### Physical Preparation

| Area | Actions |
|------|---------|
| **Sleep** | Target 8+ hours the night before; avoid late-night screen time; establish a pre-sleep routine |
| **Nutrition** | Eat a familiar, carbohydrate-stable meal before the match; avoid experimenting with new foods match morning; hydrate consistently |
| **Physical warm-up** | Light movement to reduce physical stiffness; mobility work if relevant; nothing that produces fatigue |
| **Injury awareness** | Note any physical conditions that may affect grip, stance, or endurance; communicate to coach if relevant |

### Mental and Emotional Preparation

| Area | Actions |
|------|---------|
| **Visualization** | Full stage mental rehearsal — run each stage in your mind at execution speed; include transitions, reset, and stop plate |
| **Positive cueing** | Review your two or three most reliable technical cues; focus on process, not outcome |
| **Goal setting** | Define what a successful day looks like in terms of process (e.g., "clean first shots," "visual lead on every transition") |
| **Emotional baseline** | Acknowledge nervousness as normal; set an expectation for how you will respond to a bad stage |

### Logistical Preparation

| Area | Actions |
|------|---------|
| **Gear inspection** | Check firearm function, cleanliness, and adjustment; verify holster fit and retention; confirm ammunition count |
| **Ammunition** | Load magazines to match capacity requirements; verify load and caliber; check for defects |
| **Support equipment** | Hearing protection, eye protection, appropriate footwear, weather gear, water, snacks |
| **Team coordination** | Know squad assignment, match schedule, and any volunteer responsibilities |

### Dry-Fire and Tool-Assisted Preparation

| Tool | Application |
|------|-------------|
| **Dry-fire** | Presentation, draw, sight acquisition, and trigger press rehearsal at home before the match |
| **VR training** | Stage visualization and stage walk-through if available and appropriate |
| **Airgun** | Low-cost rehearsal of presentation and engagement sequence at home |

## Layer 2 — Stage Preparation

**Time horizon:** Minutes before a stage begins.

**Purpose:** Build a stage-specific execution plan and confirm physical and mental readiness to execute it.

Stage preparation occurs after the stage has been set and before the first string is fired.

### Stage Walk and Analysis

| Step | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| **Target identification** | Locate all targets; identify stop plate; note relative positions |
| **Target order selection** | Determine optimal target sequence (typically stop plate last) |
| **Foot position selection** | Identify starting foot position; consider whether movement mid-stage is needed |
| **Transition path planning** | Map the muzzle path from target to target; identify long transitions that need visual lead |
| **Hazard identification** | Note obstacles, props, muzzle clearance issues, or irregular target angles |

### Mental Rehearsal

| Technique | Application |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Full run visualization** | Stand in the shooting position and mentally run the stage at execution speed |
| **Walk-through** | Physically step through the stage while mentally or physically simulating the sequence |
| **Transition emphasis** | Mentally rehearse the one or two transitions most likely to cause hesitation |
| **Stop plate plan** | Explicitly plan your stop plate approach — never leave it as an afterthought |

### Airgun and Physical Rehearsal

| Tool | Application |
|------|-------------|
| **Airgun walk-through** | Use airgun to simulate the engagement sequence and practice transitions on the actual stage |
| **Empty-gun rehearsal** | Simulate presentation and transitions with empty firearm to internalize the stage map |

## Layer 3 — String Preparation

**Time horizon:** Seconds before the start signal.

**Purpose:** Establish the physical and mental state required to execute the plan.

String preparation is the immediate pre-execution routine. It is brief, consistent, and deliberate.

> **See also:** [String Preparation definition in terminology.md](../doctrine/terminology.md#string-preparation)

### Physical Setup

| Element | Cue |
|---------|-----|
| **Foot position** | Confirm starting foot position is set |
| **Grip** | Establish grip before low-ready or holster; verify pressure is correct |
| **Stance** | Check weight distribution and balance |
| **Ready position** | Confirm appropriate starting position (holster, low-ready, high-ready) |

### Mental Setup

| Element | Cue |
|---------|-----|
| **Stage plan recall** | Run the stage plan one more time — target order, transitions, stop plate |
| **First-target focus** | Shift focus to the first target; the plan is done, execution begins |
| **Breathing** | One controlled breath out; settle into a calm, alert state |
| **Single cue activation** | Pick one technical cue for this string (e.g., "visual lead" or "prep during movement") |
| **Commitment** | Commit to the plan; hesitation at the signal is a string prep failure |

## Preparation Tools Reference

| Tool | Layer | Best Application |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| Sleep | Match Prep | Physical and cognitive recovery before competition |
| Nutrition/Hydration | Match Prep | Consistent energy; avoid blood sugar crashes during multi-stage matches |
| Visualization | Match Prep, Stage Prep | Mental rehearsal of execution sequences and transitions |
| VR training | Match Prep, Stage Prep | Spatial rehearsal and stage walk-through simulation |
| Dry-fire | Match Prep | Presentation, draw, and trigger discipline at home |
| Airgun | Match Prep, Stage Prep | Live-fire-like rehearsal with minimal noise and cost |
| Stage walk | Stage Prep | Physical target familiarization and transition planning |
| Breathing | String Prep | Nervous system settling immediately before execution |
| Positive cueing | Match Prep, String Prep | Technical confidence activation |

## Preparation Philosophy

Preparation is a trainable skill. It is also one of the most commonly under-coached skills in youth shooting sports.

Key principles:

- **Preparation is where confidence is built, not found.** Athletes who arrive at a match hoping to feel confident are too late. Confidence is the product of having prepared.
- **Routine reduces cognitive load.** A consistent pre-string routine means the athlete does not have to think about how to prepare. The routine runs itself, freeing cognitive resources for execution.
- **Preparation quality shows up in early strings.** The first string of a stage often reflects preparation quality more than technical skill. Athletes who rush preparation show it on their first shot.
- **Cheap rehearsal beats no rehearsal.** Airgun and dry-fire rehearsal are low-cost approximations of live-fire preparation. They are not perfect — but they are dramatically better than no preparation.
