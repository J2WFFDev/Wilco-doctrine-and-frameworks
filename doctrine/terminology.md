# Canonical Terminology

> **Rule:** This is the single source of truth for all program terminology. Other documents link here — they do not redefine these terms. If a term is missing or incorrect, update it here.

---

## Performance & Execution Terms

### Impact Assessment
The real-time evaluation of where a shot landed relative to the intended point of impact. Used to determine whether the shooter's call matched the outcome and whether a correction is needed.

### Shot Calling
The shooter's declaration of where they believe the shot landed at the moment of the shot break — before looking at the target. Accurate shot calling indicates strong visual focus and sight picture maintenance through the shot.

### Trigger Prep
The application of controlled, progressive pressure to the trigger prior to the final firing sequence. Trigger prep eliminates slack and stages the trigger for a clean break without disturbing sight alignment.

### Trigger Reset
The intentional forward release of the trigger to the reset point following a shot, in preparation for the next shot. Proper reset ensures consistency and prevents trigger slap.

### Shot Break
The moment the firearm discharges. Specifically, the quality of the shot break — whether it was clean (no movement) or disturbed (flinch, anticipation, push).

### Predictive Execution
A firing mode in which the shooter fires based on an anticipated sight picture, typically used in SASP when the shot is fired as the muzzle arrives on target. High-risk, high-reward; requires a well-developed presentation and strong internalized target geometry.

### Confirmed Execution
A firing mode in which the shooter fires only after visually confirming an acceptable sight picture on the target. Safer, more accurate, but slower than predictive execution.

### Transition Efficiency
The speed and accuracy with which a shooter moves from one target to the next. Measured by the time from the shot break on one target to the first shot break on the next, relative to the distance and difficulty of the transition.

### Presentation
The complete movement from holster (or low-ready) to the point where the firearm is on target and the shooter is ready to fire. Includes draw, grip, stance, and sight alignment establishment.

## Run Classification Terms

### Clean Run
A stage run in which all targets were engaged successfully, no procedural errors occurred, and execution was consistent with the shooter's trained process. May not be the fastest run.

### Sloppy Run
A stage run in which hits were achieved but execution deviated from the trained process — through anticipation, poor transitions, or inconsistent trigger work. May be fast but is not a reliable model to repeat.

### Recovery Run
A stage run in which one or more errors occurred (miss, procedural fault, equipment issue) and the shooter successfully recognized and corrected the error during the string. Quality of recovery is evaluated, not the raw time.

## Stage & Match Terms

### Stage Preparation
The process of analyzing a stage before shooting — walking the stage, identifying target order, planning transitions, selecting foot positions, and rehearsing the plan mentally or physically.

### String Preparation
The immediate pre-firing routine performed before each string within a stage. Includes establishing stance, grip, mental readiness, and calling the shot plan.

### PARR
**Prepare · Act · Review · Repeat**
The four-phase performance cycle used at all levels of Wilco training. PARR structures both individual runs and longer development cycles.

| Phase | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Prepare** | Stage walk, mental rehearsal, string setup |
| **Act** | Execute the string or stage with full commitment |
| **Review** | Honest assessment of what happened vs. what was planned |
| **Repeat** | Apply the insight from the review into the next attempt |

### OKR
**Objectives and Key Results**
A goal-setting framework used to define what the athlete is working toward (Objective) and how progress will be measured (Key Results). OKRs are set at the session, match, and season level.

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Objective** | A qualitative, motivating goal statement |
| **Key Result** | A specific, measurable outcome that indicates progress |

### OGSM
**Objectives · Goals · Strategies · Measures**
A strategic planning framework used at the program level for seasonal planning and development milestones.

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Objective** | Broad directional statement |
| **Goal** | Specific, quantified target |
| **Strategy** | The approach taken to reach the goal |
| **Measure** | How progress and success are tracked |

### Split Time
The elapsed time between any two consecutive shot breaks. Measured from one shot break to the next. **What split time captures depends on context:**

- **In Test 1 (rapid fire, single target):** The split time reflects the pure trigger press cycle — engage, take up slack, wall, prep, prep, prep, break, reset. No transition or recoil management is involved. This is the athlete's mechanical trigger speed in isolation.
- **In a stage run or transition-heavy drill:** The split time between shots on different targets includes lateral muzzle movement, recoil management, and possibly direction reversals in addition to the trigger cycle.

Split time is always a raw timer measurement; interpreting it correctly requires knowing what sub-tasks occurred between the two shots.

### First Shot Time (1st Shot Time)
The elapsed time from the start signal (audible beep) to the first shot break. First Shot Time includes the athlete's neurological reaction time plus any presentation movement required (e.g., from low-ready or from holster), plus the trigger cycle needed to fire the first shot. First Shot Time is recorded separately from subsequent split times.

> **Legacy alias:** Initial Shot Time (IST). The program standard term is now **First Shot Time**.

### Reaction Time
The neurological component of First Shot Time — the delay between receiving the start signal and the beginning of a physical response. Calculated by subtracting the average split time (or fastest split time) from First Shot Time. Reaction time cannot be measured directly; it is derived from the difference between First Shot Time and the shooter's mechanical trigger speed.

> **Formula:** Reaction Time = First Shot Time − Average Split Time

### Transition Time
An umbrella term for the two distinct time components that involve moving the muzzle to a new target. Because their sub-components differ, they are named and measured separately:

#### First-Target Acquisition Time
The elapsed time from the start signal (beep) to the first shot break, measured from low-ready position. Commonly called **"1st shot time."** Contains: neurological reaction time + muzzle presentation movement from low ready to the first target + trigger press. This is typically the first and most accessible place to recover time in a clean run. Measured in **Test 3**. Derived coaching value: subtract Reaction Time and Average Split Time to isolate the presentation movement component.

#### Inter-Target Transition Time (ITT)
The elapsed time from the shot break on one target to the shot break on the next target (T1→T2, T2→T3, T3→T4, T4→T5). ITT captures the full inter-target movement cycle: lateral muzzle travel, recoil management, any direction reversals, impact confirmation, and the trigger cycle on the new target. Because each inter-target move covers a different angular distance, individual ITT values vary; the **average ITT** across all moves and multiple runs is the value used in the Theoretical Stage Time formula. Measured in **Test 2** by direction (left-to-right and right-to-left), because body geometry can produce asymmetric results.

### Theoretical Stage Time
A calculated estimate of a complete stage string time, derived by combining the athlete's measured performance components — First-Target Acquisition Time, Inter-Target Transition Time (ITT), and trigger cycle time — applied to a specific stage layout. Theoretical Stage Time can be modeled in serial or parallel execution:

- **Serial model:** First-Target Acquisition Time + (Transitions × Avg ITT) + (Follow-on shots × Avg Trigger Press Time)
- **Parallel model:** First-Target Acquisition Time + (Transitions × max(Avg ITT, Fastest Trigger Press Time))

Parallel execution compresses time by overlapping trigger prep into the transition movement. See [frameworks/execution-models.md](../frameworks/execution-models.md) and [frameworks/steel-baseline-assessment.md](../frameworks/steel-baseline-assessment.md) for full methodology.

## Equipment & Condition Terms

### Bio-Condition
The athlete's physical and mental state on a given training day or match day — including sleep, nutrition, stress, and physical readiness. Tracked as a factor when reviewing performance variance.
