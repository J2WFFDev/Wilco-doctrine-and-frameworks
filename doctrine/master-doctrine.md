# Wilco Shooting Sports Master Doctrine

## Purpose

This document establishes the foundational beliefs, principles, and operating model of Wilco Shooting Sports. It serves as the highest-authority reference in the document hierarchy. All frameworks, guides, and operational documents are subordinate to and consistent with this doctrine.

---

## Mission

To develop disciplined, skilled, and character-driven junior shooters through structured training, competitive experience, and a culture of continuous improvement.

---

## Vision

A program recognized for producing athletes who perform with precision and composure under pressure — and who leave the program as better people than when they arrived.

---

## Values

| Value | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Safety First** | No competitive goal overrides safe range behavior. Ever. |
| **Discipline** | Consistency in preparation, process, and execution. |
| **Ownership** | Athletes take responsibility for their performance and growth. |
| **Respect** | For teammates, competitors, coaches, parents, and the equipment. |
| **Improvement** | Progress over perfection; deliberate practice over repetition. |
| **Integrity** | Honest self-assessment and transparent communication. |

---

## Athlete Development Model

Athlete development in Wilco Shooting Sports follows three progressive stages:

### Stage 1 — Foundation
- Safe gun handling as an absolute baseline
- Introduction to sight picture, trigger control, and follow-through
- Familiarity with competition format and stage procedures
- Building routine and range etiquette

### Stage 2 — Development
- Structured drill work tied to performance components
- Introduction to run classification and self-assessment
- Goal-setting using PARR and OKR frameworks
- Building mental and physical pre-shot routines

### Stage 3 — Performance
- Execution under match-condition pressure
- Advanced analytics and performance tracking
- Mentorship roles and leadership development
- Independent preparation and self-coaching skills

> See [terminology.md](terminology.md) for definitions of PARR, OKR, and run classification terms.

## Coaching Philosophy

*See [philosophy.md](philosophy.md) for the full coaching philosophy.*

Key principles:

- Coaches teach process, not just outcomes.
- Feedback is specific, timely, and tied to observable behavior.
- Athletes are not managed — they are developed.
- The coach's job is to make the athlete increasingly self-sufficient.

## Safety Doctrine

Safety is non-negotiable and supersedes all other program goals.

Core rules:
1. All firearms treated as loaded at all times.
2. Muzzle never pointed at anything not intended to be shot.
3. Finger off trigger until sights are on target and decision to shoot is made.
4. Know your target and what is beyond it.

Violations result in immediate range removal and documented review. Repeated violations result in program suspension.

> Full safety procedures are in [operations/safety-manual.md](../operations/safety-manual.md).

## Reflection Model

Athletes and coaches use structured reflection to convert experience into growth:

1. **Observe** — What actually happened? (data, not interpretation)
2. **Assess** — What does this tell us about current state vs. target?
3. **Decide** — What is the one highest-leverage change?
4. **Act** — Execute the change with intention in the next session.

This cycle applies to individual runs, training sessions, match days, and seasons.
