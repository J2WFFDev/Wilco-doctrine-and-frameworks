# Purpose

This doctrine serves as the master framework for Wilco Shooting Sports.

The doctrine establishes:

- Organizational philosophy
- Culture expectations
- Safety expectations
- Athlete development principles
- Coaching philosophy
- Performance methodology
- Leadership standards
- Systems thinking models
- Terminology standards
- Reflection and continuous improvement models

This doctrine is intended to provide a stable foundational framework from which additional operational guides, training systems, manuals, and procedures may be derived.

---

# Doctrine Structure

This doctrine is intentionally structured as a stable knowledge framework.

The doctrine should contain:

- Philosophy
- Principles
- Definitions
- Systems
- Models
- Standards
- Development frameworks

The doctrine should generally avoid:

- Temporary schedules
- Seasonal logistics
- Rapidly changing procedures
- Platform-specific technical instructions
- Temporary operational workflows

Operational procedures and guides should instead derive from this doctrine.

# Safety Doctrine

Safety is integrated into all activities.

Safety expectations include:

- Muzzle discipline
- Action control
- Trigger discipline
- Range awareness
- Emotional control under stress
- Physical control against acute stress
- Respect for commands
- Responsible firearm transport and storage
- Equipment inspection and maintenance
- Safe corrective behavior

Unsafe behavior should be addressed immediately and constructively.

---

# Mental Performance and Emotional Control

Competitive shooting includes significant mental and emotional components.

Athletes should develop:

- Emotional control
- Positive recovery habits
- Confidence
- Reflection discipline
- Match composure
- Resilience after mistakes

One poor string or stage should not define an athlete.

Athletes should learn to:

1. Safely complete execution
2. Maintain emotional control
3. Reflect at the appropriate time
4. Extract lessons constructively
5. Refocus on future execution

---

# Reflection and Continuous Improvement

Improvement requires reflection.

Execution without reflection often produces repeated mistakes.

Athletes and coaches should regularly evaluate:

- What occurred
- Why it occurred
- Which variables influenced performance
- Which improvements are needed
- Which habits should be reinforced

Reflection should support growth rather than negative self-talk.

# PARR Systems Model

Wilco Shooting Sports uses a systems-oriented improvement cycle referred to as PARR.

PARR:

- Prepare
- Act
- Reflect
- Refine

---

## Prepare

Examples:

- Goal setting
- Visualization
- Match preparation
- Gear checks
- Warmups
- Airgun rehearsal
- VR rehearsal
- Sleep and nutrition preparation

---

## Act

Examples:

- Live-fire execution
- Match participation
- Drill execution
- Team contribution
- Data collection

---

## Reflect

Examples:

- Performance review
- Emotional review
- Equipment evaluation
- Reflection discussions
- Coaching review

---

## Refine

Examples:

- Technique adjustment
- Equipment adjustment
- Drill modification
- Preparation improvement
- Mental process improvement

# Preparation Framework

## Match Preparation

Preparation occurring before arriving at the match.

Examples:

- Gear inspection
- Ammunition preparation
- Visualization
- VR/Dryfire training
- Sleep preparation
- Nutrition planning
- Team coordination
- Environmental preparation

---

## Stage Preparation

Preparation occurring before a stage begins.

Examples:

- Stage planning
- Airgun training
- Mental rehearsal
- Visualization
- Environmental assessment
- Strategy selection

---

## String Preparation

Immediate preparation before execution.

Examples:

- Form alignment
- Grip confirmation
- Visual readiness
- Breathing control
- Positive cueing
- Trigger readiness
- Focus stabilization

# Terminology Guidance

The organization attempts to maintain consistent terminology across:

- Coaches
- Athletes
- Parents
- Guides
- Analytics systems
- Training materials
- Software systems

Technical terms should be clearly defined when introduced.

Examples include:

- Impact assessment
- Trigger prep
- Shot Break
- Predictive execution
- Confirmed execution
- Recovery run
- Sloppy run
- Transition efficiency

Metaphorical use of firearm-related terms should generally be avoided when clearer alternatives exist.
